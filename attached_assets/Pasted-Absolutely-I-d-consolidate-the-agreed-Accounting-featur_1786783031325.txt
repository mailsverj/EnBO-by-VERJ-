2. Solar System Design Engine

The design engine should automatically generate an initial solar solution from the BDO’s submitted site/load information.

Engineers can subsequently modify the automatically generated design.

Battery sizing

Remove the separate Backup Hours field.

Use:

* Appliance/load
* Quantity
* Power rating
* Hours/day
* Hours/night

The Hours/night value determines the energy that needs to be supplied by the battery.

Battery energy requirement should be calculated as:

Night-time energy requirement × 1.25

Where the 1.25 factor accounts for the specified 20% DOD allowance + 5% inefficiency.

In practical terms:

Battery Required (kWh) = Total Night-time Load Energy (kWh) × 1.25

The system should calculate the night-time energy from the individual appliance entries rather than asking the BDO to manually enter backup hours.

3. Battery Selection Logic

After calculating required battery storage, BuDOM should select the appropriate available battery module.

Required Storage	Selected Battery
0–5 kWh	5 kWh
5.1–10 kWh	10 kWh
10.1–16 kWh	16 kWh
16.1–32 kWh	32 kWh
32.1–48 kWh	48 kWh
Above 48 kWh	Continue the progression using available battery modules

The system should select the smallest available battery configuration that meets or exceeds the calculated requirement.

Multiple battery modules can be combined where necessary.

⸻

4. PV Sizing

Use the agreed PV sizing methodology:

PV Required = (Total Battery Size ÷ 6 hours + Total Load) × 1.67

The 1.67 factor represents the specified allowance for approximately 40% system inefficiency.

The design engine should clearly distinguish:

* Calculated PV requirement
* Selected PV panel wattage
* Number of panels
* Total installed PV capacity

⸻

5. Inverter Selection

The inverter selection engine should use the agreed load/PV progression.

The system should:

1. Determine required inverter capacity from total load.
2. Check whether the calculated PV capacity requires the next inverter class.
3. Select the appropriate inverter module from available inventory.
4. If the exact intended inverter size isn’t available, select the nearest available size, provided the difference does not exceed 5 kW from the intended capacity.
5. If no acceptable inverter exists within the 5 kW tolerance, flag the design for engineer review rather than silently selecting an unsuitable inverter.

The progression should be scalable beyond the currently defined sizes.
